China Growth Roadmap

From RMB 0 to RMB 150M ARR

A detailed, China-only scaling plan for Lulu Growth OS based on an annual price of RMB 30,000 per customer / factory. This version is intentionally more detailed and uses a 36-month horizon, because RMB 150M ARR requires very different execution logic than the earlier RMB 10M roadmap.

Market: China only
Price: RMB 30,000 / year
Target: RMB 150,000,000 ARR
Horizon: 36 months

Executive Summary

At RMB 30,000 ARR per customer, Lulu needs 5,000 active paying customers to reach RMB 150M ARR. This is not a broad self-serve SaaS story. It is a focused industrial GTM strategy built around vertical packaging, factory groups, regional clusters, annual prepay, partner channels, and land-and-expand execution.

Detailed Roadmap by Phase

Phase 1

0 -> 1.5M

Months 1-6

  • Choose one beachhead vertical: for example export-focused manufacturers with English websites and overseas demand goals.
  • Turn Lulu into a clear offer: not “AI software”, but “AI growth system for export manufacturers”.
  • Founder-led sales: close the first 50 customers directly or very close to the founder.
  • Create 5 proof assets: before/after case studies, live dashboards, visible search wins, faster content publishing, measurable lead improvements.
  • Build a low-friction onboarding motion: if setup is too manual, later scale will break.

Phase 2

1.5M -> 7.5M

Months 7-12

  • Standardize the sales engine: ICP list building, outreach scripts, demo narrative, proposal template, annual prepay enforcement.
  • Open 2-3 channel motions: website agencies, export consultants, B2B digital operators, industrial SaaS resellers.
  • Focus by cluster, not by country-wide spray: Shenzhen, Dongguan, Foshan, Ningbo, Suzhou, Wuxi, Qingdao, and Guangzhou are more practical than “all of China”.
  • Reach 250 total customers: this is the first proof that demand is not purely founder-dependent.
  • Track the right metrics: demo-to-close, onboarding time, activation rate, 90-day retention, partner-originated pipeline share.

Phase 3

7.5M -> 22.5M

Months 13-18

  • Double down on what closes fastest: one vertical can become 60-70% of revenue early, and that is fine.
  • Expand from single-site wins to group wins: one signed manufacturing group should turn into multiple factories and brands.
  • Turn case studies into a growth system: WeChat content, founder talks, cluster events, private industry circles, direct introductions.
  • Reach 750 total customers: this is where operational efficiency becomes as important as raw selling.
  • Start a structured customer success layer: renewals, expansion, deeper adoption, and proof collection.

Phase 4

22.5M -> 60M

Months 19-24

  • Switch from “sell product” to “roll out program”: a cluster-level and partner-led implementation model becomes necessary.
  • Build a stronger mid-market team: regional sales leads, partner managers, onboarding managers, customer success managers.
  • Reach 2,000 total customers: now Lulu begins to look like an industrial platform rather than an early-stage product.
  • Protect quality: poor onboarding or weak activation here will create later churn cliffs.
  • Measure expansion revenue explicitly: group-level upsells, additional sites, and broader automation packages.

Phase 5

60M -> 105M

Months 25-30

  • Compound growth through channels: at this stage, partners and referrals should generate a meaningful share of new business.
  • Vertical playbooks should now be productized: each new factory should not feel like a custom project.
  • Reach 3,500 total customers: which means selling scale and operational trust, not just features.
  • Strengthen retention reporting: logo retention, gross revenue retention, expansion ARR, payback period.
  • Build board-grade data discipline: because once numbers get large, credibility matters more than narrative.

Phase 6

105M -> 150M

Months 31-36

  • Reach 5,000 active customers: this is the direct numeric path to RMB 150M ARR at the current base price.
  • Retention now matters more than velocity alone: if churn rises, the last stretch gets much harder.
  • Balance acquisition and account expansion: new logos alone should not carry the whole final step.
  • Institutionalize partner governance: enablement, revenue share, quality control, and renewal ownership.
  • Prepare next-step monetization: higher-tier packages, add-ons, implementation fees, group contracts, and executive analytics.

Recommended China GTM Architecture

Direct Sales

Best for the first 100-250 customers and the most strategic anchor accounts.

Channel Partners

Needed to scale beyond founder bandwidth, especially in industrial clusters.

Cluster Strategy

Go deep in a few manufacturing regions before broad geographic expansion.

Group Expansion

One manufacturing group should become multiple factories, brands, or business units.

Funnel Math Needed to Support 5,000 Customers

Top Funnel

  • qualified pipeline must be far larger than raw lead volume
  • case-study marketing should create trust before the first demo
  • partner-sourced pipeline should rise materially after year 1

Mid Funnel

  • demo narrative must stay vertical-specific
  • annual contract logic should be non-negotiable by default
  • onboarding promise must be simple and believable

Bottom Funnel

  • activation within 30-45 days should be treated as a board KPI
  • renewal proof must start in month 2, not month 11
  • group expansion should be planned at contract signature

Top Risks

  1. Trying to sell to all manufacturers instead of one strong vertical first
  2. Allowing a low-quality monthly pricing model to dilute cash discipline
  3. Turning every customer into a custom service project
  4. Weak onboarding causing low activation and future churn
  5. Building partner channels too early without clear enablement and economics

Reality check: RMB 150M ARR at the current base price is not a “marketing campaign” outcome. It requires a true industrial growth machine with disciplined packaging, strong product operations, repeatable cluster GTM, and expansion-led account strategy.

Recommended Operating Sequence

1. Nail one wedge

One use case, one vertical, one strong result narrative.

2. Prove ROI

Turn the first 50 wins into visible case studies and repeatable trust assets.

3. Build channels

Use agencies, consultants, and cluster partners after the message already converts.

4. Expand groups

Scale through multi-site and multi-brand rollouts, not just new single logos.
